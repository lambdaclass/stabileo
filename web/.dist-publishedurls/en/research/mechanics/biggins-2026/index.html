Snapping arches by curvature patterning

After Michał Zmyślony, Ammar Khan, John S. Biggins,“Snapping and switching of elastic arches with patterned preferred curvature”, Physical Review Research (2026), doi:10.1103/56f8-8gsx.

Change the width of a curved segment and move the threshold at which a shallow arch switches sides.

From Euler buckling to an equilibrium jump

A flat-clamped arch stores end shortening in its shape. A preferred-curvature pattern couples to its buckling modes. Equations (2) and (3), arXiv v2 pages 3–4, give the shallow-angle equilibrium and compression spectrum. We restrict this experiment to a centered rectangular pattern.

The production Stabileo 2D frame buckling solver computes 16 modes on 64 elements. A research layer projects the curvature onto these modes and solves the paper’s compression constraint. The same Rust code runs here as WASM and generates the replay.

·

Validation and sources

At a stimulated width of 70%, the paper gives α/√ε = 21.52 (arXiv v2 page 3, following Eq. 2). The overlay is regenerated from the authors’ shallow-angle software in Zenodo record 18256271, licensed CC-BY-4.0. It is a numerical reference, not experimental validation.

Paper and supplementary equations · Zenodo data and software

The authors’ default optimizer and rectangular-profile quadrature produce small irregularities; their 70% output is 21.5141, while the paper reports 21.52.

This is a shallow, inextensible equilibrium model. The jump connects equilibria; it does not simulate snap dynamics or reproduce laboratory measurements. Arc-length in the production engine rejects prescribed displacements and thermal loading, so it is not used for this curvature-driven path. Only centered patterns and small end shortening are covered.
