MacNeal–Harder (1985): engine benchmarks

After Richard H. Macneal, Robert L. Harder,“A proposed standard set of problems to test finite element accuracy”, Finite Elements in Analysis and Design (1985), doi:10.1016/0168-874x(85)90003-4.

Against the author’s code
– No separate reference solver: this page runs the production engine.
Against the paper’s figures
– The six MITC4 shell cases are compared with the paper’s tip displacements 0.4321 and 0.1081 (Table 3, p. 10, as given in a public transcription), which Timoshenko beam theory reproduces to all four digits. The six frame cases are compared with Euler–Bernoulli formulas, not with the paper.

Research, not a design tool. Nothing here is an engineering result.

The production Stabileo engine, run in this browser on MacNeal–Harder straight-cantilever cases and on analytical frame cases. Every result is computed live; failures stay visible.

What is tested

MacNeal and Harder proposed accuracy problems for finite elements. This page runs their straight cantilever with regular, trapezoidal and parallelogram meshes of MITC4 shells, loaded out of plane and in plane. Six Euler–Bernoulli frame cases, analytical adaptations rather than problems from the paper, are reported separately. The rest of the published set (curved and twisted beams, plates, shells, thick cylinder) is not on this page.

Units are kN and m. The shell strip is 6 m long, 0.2 m wide and 0.1 m thick, with Young’s modulus 10⁷ kN/m² and Poisson’s ratio 0.3, meshed with six elements and clamped at the root; a 1 kN tip force is split equally between the two tip nodes. The engine reads modulus in MPa, so the case file stores 10 000 (×1000 = 10⁷ kN/m²). The frame cases are 6 m cantilevers with E = 200 000 MPa, A = 0.01 m² and I = 10⁻⁴ m⁴, under a 1 kN tip force or a 10 kN·m tip moment.

One benchmark definition

The engine-owned case file holds the inputs, measurement locations, signed references, expected outcomes and sources. Native tests and this page read it. No research solver supplies these results.

Accuracy criterion

A case passes when its relative error is below 2%. That limit is a Stabileo choice, not a tolerance from the paper. Results and references are signed displacements or rotations in global axes, so a response in the wrong direction fails. The paper prints shell references to four digits; when the verdict would change somewhere within half a unit of the last digit, the row says marginal, as for the regular out-of-plane case at 1.99%. The Research workflow runs the same cases natively and in WebAssembly, requires identical values and verdicts, and pins the expected outcome of every case. A case the engine withholds has no value or error and never counts as a pass.

Known limitations

Low-order elements on distorted meshes can lock, and failing or withheld cases stay in the count. The engine test ledger documents the two failures on trapezoidal and parallelogram meshes loaded out of plane, and the two in-plane cases on those meshes, which the engine withholds (diagnostic SH-MITC4-DISTORTION), all tracked as issue #798. Only those rows link to it; any other failure is shown as an unexpected mismatch or an engine error. Native and WebAssembly runs must both withhold the two in-plane cases. This page does not change engine code.

Where the references come from

Geometry is the paper’s Fig. 4 (p. 7). The shell references 0.4321 and 0.1081 are the theoretical tip displacements in the load direction in Table 3 (p. 10), according to a public transcription of the paper; we have not inspected the publisher’s scan. A native test recomputes both from the case inputs with Timoshenko beam theory (shear factor 5/6): 0.43209 and 0.10809, the printed values to four digits. The frame references are PL³/(3EI), ML²/(2EI) and ML/(EI).
