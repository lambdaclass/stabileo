One five-node example from Fairclough and colleagues: four supports, one central load, and four curved compression members.

The reproduced example

Table 1, first row: a unit square supported at its four corners, a unit downward load at the centre, unit compressive strength, and normalized unit weight ρgL/σ = 1.65. The ten possible connections form the fixed ground structure. Clarabel solves the second-order cone relaxation from equations (19)–(20), (29); four connections carry compression. Elevations and equal-stress centrelines are recovered after the solve.

What this establishes

The checks compare volume, active members, forces, section areas, centreline coordinates, apex elevation and conic feasibility. The printed volume is checked to half a unit in its last decimal place. Agreement with reference code is not experimental validation or engineering certification.

Reaction/load bookkeeping and editor

The curved result is exported as 128 straight 3D frame segments with circular sections, corner pins, E = 1000 MPa and ν = 0.3. Explicit nodal loads approximate continuous self-weight. The production engine checks linear static response and vertical force balance. Bending stiffness stabilizes this frame surrogate; it is not the paper’s compression-only catenary model. The editor opens this same model. Automatic self-weight is off to avoid counting it twice.

Engine vertical balance · relative error: 0.0000000000138

Scope limits

Only the five-node row at this one unit weight is reproduced. The other Table 1 rows, the 11×11 grid, the optimized 13-node layouts, member adding, position search, counterweights, holes, and the full general catenary layout formulation are out of scope. No buckling, nonlinear stability, joints, design checks or experimental validation are claimed.
