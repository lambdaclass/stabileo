# Navier plate

Route: `/research/benchmarks/navier-plate`. One simply supported rectangular plate under uniform
pressure, solved by two production elements (DKT and MITC4) through the production WASM engine,
against the double Fourier series of Navier's solution and against a published worked example.
No research solver and no engine numerics changes. Research, not a design tool.

The historical label "Navier (1823)" names the classical problem; it is not asserted as a verified
publication date. The catalogue cites the Crossref-verified modern chapter, Ventsel and
Krauthammer, *Rectangular Plates* (2001), DOI 10.1201/9780203908723-3. The published numbers the
page compares with come from Terje Haukaas, *Theory of Plates and Shells, Article 28, Navier's
Solution for Uniform Load* (University of British Columbia, updated 9 February 2018), a worked
example of Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger (1959), Article 28. The UBC address
(<https://civil-terje.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2019/06/Naviers-Plate-Solution-for-Uniform-Load.pdf>)
now answers automated clients with a browser check; the values below were read from the Internet
Archive's copy of that PDF. No source code, text or figure was copied.

## The example

3 × 5 m, thickness 0.1 m, E = 63,000 MPa, ν = 0.2, pressure 10 kN/m² downward (Haukaas p. 1:
a = 3, b = 5, q0 = 10, h = 0.1, E = 63 000 000, ν = 0.2 in kN and m). All four edges have uz = 0
and free bending rotations (soft simple support). At (0,0), ux = uy = 0; at (3,0), uy = 0. The
pressure is lumped as tributary nodal forces, cell by cell (qA/3 per triangle node, qA/4 per
quadrilateral node), with no consistent rotational terms; the total load is 150 kN. DKT splits
each rectangle along its ascending diagonal; MITC4 uses one quadrilateral. The page's six cases
are 4×4, 8×8 and 16×16 subdivisions of each family.

`engine/tests/benchmarks/navier_plate.json` holds the six explicit solver inputs, owned by the
engine; its native test rebuilds every case from the plate definition and requires the fixture to
equal it. `web/src/components/research/navier1823/model.ts` ports that builder operation for
operation; Vitest and `wasm/check.mjs` require it to reproduce the six fixture cases byte for byte.
`results/stabileo_results.json` is the native production run (replay), regenerated by the native
test with `NAVIER_RESULTS`.

## Interactive

The page autoplays the replay of the six paper meshes, recomputes them live in a terminable worker
(Recompute live, Stop, Restart), and lets the reader change the plate: element (DKT or MITC4),
support (soft, or hard with the rotation along each edge also restrained), mesh divisions per side
(2 to 32, even, so the centre is a node) and thickness (0.02 to 0.5 m). Each change builds that
plate with the ported builder and solves it with production WASM in the browser. The reference
shown follows the plate: the thin-plate series for DKT, the Reissner–Mindlin series for MITC4 with
hard support, and none for MITC4 with soft support (see below). Links in the article set these
parameters.

## Two series

- **Thin-plate (Kirchhoff) series**, 100 × 100 odd terms: 1.2837481142 mm. 400 × 400 terms move it
  by 2.6e-11 mm. The centre sine factors alternate; summing their absolute values would be wrong.
- **Reissner–Mindlin series, hard simple support**, shear correction κ = 5/6, G = E/2(1 + ν),
  1000 × 1000 odd terms: 1.288120842251 mm (+0.341% over thin plate). The shear term is
  16q/(π⁴κGt) Σ sin(mπ/2) sin(nπ/2) / [mn(m²/a² + n²/b²)]. 2000 × 2000 terms move it by 7e-13 mm.

Both are evaluated independently in Rust (`navier_plate.rs`) and TypeScript (`model.ts`). For a
thickness other than 0.1 m the page evaluates the Reissner–Mindlin series with 200 × 200 terms.

DKT is a thin-plate element and converges to the Kirchhoff series at second order. MITC4 includes
transverse shear; with only uz restrained at the edges (soft support) it solves a different problem
with an edge boundary layer: its centre deflection keeps rising above both series under refinement
(32×32: 1.305399 mm, 64×64: 1.310611 mm), at first order. Neither series is its reference, so the
4×4 and 8×8 "closer to the thin-plate series" result is a sign crossover, not accuracy, and the
page says so. No published soft-support reference is used. With hard support MITC4 converges at
second order to the Reissner–Mindlin series.

## The paper against Stabileo

`wasm/check.mjs` writes `reference-results/published_comparison.json` (hashed in
`reference-results/MANIFEST.json`); the page shows it through the shared comparison table.

| Quantity | Source | Paper | Stabileo | Difference | Tolerance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Flexural rigidity D of the case inputs (kN·m) | p. 2 | 5468.75 | 5468.750000000002 | 1.8e-12 | 0.005 absolute |
| Centre displacement, thin-plate series, 100 × 100 odd terms (mm) | p. 3 | 1.28375 | 1.2837481142 | 1.9e-6 | 5e-6 absolute |
| Centre displacement, production DKT 16 × 16 (mm) | p. 3 | 1.28375 | 1.2809214939 | 0.2203% | 0.25% relative |
| Centre displacement, DKT Richardson extrapolation of 16 × 16 and 32 × 32 (mm) | p. 3 | 1.28375 | 1.2837470641 | 0.00023% | 0.01% relative |

Tolerances: the example prints D to two decimals and the displacement to five (half a unit of the
last digit is 0.005 and 5e-6); the 0.25% bound is the existing DKT 16×16 bound (measured 0.2202%
against the series, which is its discretisation error, as the Richardson row shows); the 0.01%
bound is the existing Richardson bound of the native test. The example evaluates the series with
10 × 10 terms; its printed value agrees with our 100 × 100 sum within its rounding.

Not compared: the example's bending moments at the centre (Mxx = 7.81945, p. 4; Myy = 3.65574,
p. 5) and the corner force 2|Mxy| = 9.14041 (p. 5). The production engine returns plate moments at
element centroids, not at the plate centre or corners, so a comparison would need an extrapolation
whose tolerance we cannot justify yet.

## Validation

`wasm/check.mjs` runs the page's own modules through Vite with the production WASM engine and
writes `VALIDATION.json`. Every check, its bound and why:

| Check | Bound | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| TypeScript builder against the six fixture cases | 0 differing | the page builds new plates with it |
| WASM against the native replay, every node, six soft and three hard meshes | 1e-9 mm | same engine, two builds (observed 0) |
| Vertical equilibrium, every mesh | 1e-6 kN | the native test's bound |
| Thin-plate series against its stored value / truncation 400 vs 100 terms | 1e-12 / 1e-9 mm | measured 3e-15 / 2.6e-11 |
| Reissner–Mindlin series against its stored value / truncation 2000 vs 1000 terms | 1e-11 / 1e-9 mm | measured 4e-13 / 7e-13 |
| Published 1.28375 mm against the thin-plate series | 5e-6 mm | the example prints five decimals |
| DKT and MITC4-hard errors decrease with every refinement | 0 violations | native test |
| DKT 16×16 against the thin-plate series | 0.25% | measured 0.2202% (discretisation error) |
| MITC4 hard 16×16 against the Reissner–Mindlin series | 0.16% | measured 0.1423% |
| DKT increment ratio (4→8)/(8→16), MITC4 hard (8→16)/(16→32) | 4 ± 0.5 | second order; the native test's 3.5–4.5 |
| Richardson extrapolation (16, 32) against its series, DKT and MITC4 hard | 0.01% | native test, measured < 0.0002% |
| MITC4 soft 32×32 and 64×64 against the documented 1.305399383 and 1.310611471 mm | 1e-6 relative | regression pins for the page's statement |
| MITC4 soft keeps rising (8, 16, 32, 64) and ends above 1.015 × Reissner–Mindlin | 0 violations | the page's statement |

The 5% bound of the first version (each case's `tolerance_relative` in the fixture) remains in the
native test. These are verification checks against exact series and a published worked example,
not an experimental validation or an engineering certification.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, after building the engine WASM into `web/src/lib/wasm` (`make wasm`, or
`wasm-pack` as that target does) and `npm ci` in `web`:

```sh
node research/benchmarks/navier-plate/wasm/check.mjs          # recompute and compare with the committed evidence
node research/benchmarks/navier-plate/wasm/check.mjs --write  # rewrite VALIDATION.json, the comparison and its manifest
cargo test --locked --manifest-path engine/Cargo.toml --test validation navier_plate_shared_cases
NAVIER_RESULTS="$PWD/research/benchmarks/navier-plate/results/stabileo_results.json" cargo test --locked --manifest-path engine/Cargo.toml --test validation navier_plate_shared_cases -- --nocapture
(cd web && npx vitest run src/components/research/navier1823)
```

The last-but-one command regenerates the replay. The gallery image is generated from the computed
contour with `python3 -I research/benchmarks/navier-plate/thumbnail.py`. The viewer shows a flat
displacement contour, not a deformed surface.
