# Concurrent secondary shear in torsion section limits (2026-11-15)

Fix #2142 corrects an unsafe omission in the CIRSOC 201 solid rectangular member verifier, in both supported editions. The section check received only primary shear and torque even though the concurrent demand retained the other shear. A column or wall could pass its separate secondary-shear reinforcement check and still exceed the concrete-section shear–torsion limit in that orientation. Beams carrying a secondary shear had the same section omission.

The source is [CIRSOC 201-2025 §22.7.7.1(a) and C22.7.7.1, page 435](../codes/CIRSOC/markdown/cirsoc-201-2025/capitulo-22-resistencia-de-las-secciones-de-los-elementos.md#c22.7.7.1). For a solid section, the left side is `sqrt((Vu/(bw*d))² + (Tu*ph/(1.7*Aoh²))²)`; its limit is `phi*(Vc/(bw*d) + 0.66*sqrt(fc))`. The commentary explains the square-root combination for solids and distinguishes the linear sum required for hollow sections. This verifier supports solid rectangles, with no hollow-section geometry or wall-thickness input; hollow sections remain outside its scope. No hollow formula is applied to a solid section.

The shared torsion module now requires the branded `ConcurrentSectionDemand` from #2080 and both effective depths. It receives no scalar `Vu`/`Tu` substitute. The section limit uses the larger of `abs(Vu)/(b*d)` and `abs(VuSecondary)/(h*dSecondary)` with the concurrent torque. This is exactly the worse of the two orientation checks: the torsion stress and concrete stress limit are unchanged by turning the rectangle. The module returns both combined and shear-only section utilizations, so the member designer obtains its limiting torque without manufacturing another demand with zero torque. The provided-stirrup checker also includes the secondary orientation's required shear steel. A separate shared provision function supplies actual stirrup steel without inventing a load case.

The provided-beam verification sweep now evaluates torsion before the primary-shear early exit. Thus zero primary shear cannot suppress the torsion check when torque and secondary shear remain. Direct, prepared, concurrent, auto, unified and design verification all reach the complete-demand section calculation. No Rust analysis or section kernel changes. Native RC checks do not implement torsion and already disclose that it is not rechecked; there is no parallel native torsion implementation to update. Real 3D linear and P-Delta WASM results are tested with compact and grouped station transfer. Real 2D linear and P-Delta results remain answered controls; 2D has no torsion degree of freedom.

The [recorded numerical comparison](2142-torsion-secondary-shear-diff.json) includes origin/main `44966b6e75f72ce268ec87798d5cd880a6dba144`, the corrected result and unchanged controls, in both editions. The exact issue witness is a column with b=0.30 m, h=0.60 m, cover=0.025 m, fc=25 MPa, fy=420 MPa, 8 mm stirrups, Mu=20 kN·m, Nu=0, primary shear=0, secondary shear=100 kN and torque=57 kN·m. The independent clause calculation gives combined-stress utilization 1.0033550542199214, allowable torque 56.800935097837595 kN·m and torque utilization 1.0035046060741697. The designer reports torque utilization, which differs from the combined-stress utilization away from unity.

With explicitly sufficient fixed steel (four stirrup legs at 0.05 m and additional longitudinal torsion allocation 100 cm²), main returns torsion and overall status `ok`, allowable torque 58.055253822359695 kN·m and utilization 0.9818232846662145. The fix returns `fail`, allowable torque 56.80093509783761 kN·m and utilization 1.0035046060741695. Main's result is silently unsafe at the section limit even with reinforcement fully declared. The coordinator determines any advisory; this change adds none. Stirrup additions cannot repair this section failure.

At primary shear=100 kN, secondary shear=0 and torque=30 kN·m, both editions retain main's full torsion result exactly, including allowable torque 56.97985938247517 kN·m, utilization 0.5265018258228074, transverse and longitudinal requirements and calculation steps.

The provided-report boundary inputs target representable utilizations 0.999 and 1.001 because that existing report rounds displayed ratios to three decimals; their statuses still use unrounded utilization.
