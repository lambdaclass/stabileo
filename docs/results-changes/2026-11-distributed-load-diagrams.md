# Distributed-load diagrams without intensity slopes (#2223)

The shared transverse distributed-load recovery formed `(qJ - qI) / (b - a)` even at stations before the loaded interval. With `a = 2^-20`, `b = a + 2^-28`, `qI = 0`, `qJ = 2^1000` on a member of length `3`, the slope overflowed while the integrated force and moment remained finite. Multiplication by the zero loaded length produced NaN before the load. Native scalar recovery returned NaN; the WASM finite-result boundary withheld the diagram explicitly.

Recovery now returns an exact zero contribution at and before `a`. Inside the load, it integrates the endpoint contributions using the bounded fraction of the loaded interval. Past the interval, it transports the accumulated resultant and interval moment to the station. No intensity slope or intensity difference is formed. This also retains the interval moment of opposite intensities downstream when the resultant is zero, rather than subtracting large station moments.

The shared helper is used by planar shear and moment recovery and both spatial bending planes. Diagrams, envelopes, station extraction, section stresses and consumers of those recovered quantities can change. The linear and nonlinear equilibrium kernels, assembled loads, interval validity and acceptance limits are unchanged. Plastic-collapse searches, moving-load extrema, influence responses and design demands that consume recovered diagrams can also change. Non-finite final outputs retain their existing refusal. Rerun affected diagrams and derived checks. The `distributed-load-diagram-integration` registry entry is limited to transverse distributed member loads, self-weight and their dependent results; unloaded, nodal-only, axial-only and point-load-only models without self-weight do not qualify. Its quantity declarations cover recovery outputs and refusal status only; they cannot excuse unrelated changes to stored solve results. No numerical baseline is updated.

The reproducer uses a saved force record with zero starting forces to isolate the load contribution. Native tests cover the common kernel, scalar and sorted planar recovery, both spatial planes and complete diagrams. WASM tests use saved records from linear, load-session, P–Delta, corotational and fiber results in both dimensions and cover the downstream recovery boundaries. Independent quadrature checks the interior moment, while the specified trapezoid resultant transports that moment downstream. Ordinary intensities, uniform, triangular and opposite endpoint intensities, stations on both sides of the load endpoints and existing geometry-limit refusal tests provide controls.

The failing self-weight notice test was a separate fixture error. PRO files without a saved self-weight rule use the legacy toggle during migration and retain the force-summary notice when enabled. An explicit empty rule suppresses that legacy interpretation in PRO; an explicit nonempty rule retains applicability. The corrected regression checks these cases alongside Basic 2D and 3D without changing production notice behavior.
