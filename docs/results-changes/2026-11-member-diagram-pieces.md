# Member extrema from load intervals

The planar moving-load envelope uses each solved position's load boundaries, jump faces and stationary points. The spatial moving-load sweep folds the right-hand limit at I, the left-hand limit at J and both faces of each interior jump. An axle that matches a stored path endpoint is applied at the corresponding member end even if subtracting cumulative distances would produce an interior coordinate. The sweep still evaluates the requested discrete train positions.

The merge with #2582 keeps the exact member intervals and adds the one-sided axle contributions to the inward cuts when a sweep samples a path joint. A simply supported span reaches the axle weight in its shear envelope at either support face. At each exact support position, the reaction carries that weight and the member forces at the sample are zero. The planar pointwise envelope keeps the two ordinates at an interior jump and takes the member-side ordinate at each end for shear and axial force.

The planar overlay scale and calculation report tables use the extrema of the same piecewise diagrams. The single-case planar report takes both end values from the same inward cuts used for the peak, immediately after a jump at I and immediately before a jump at J. A partial line load that changes from +128 to −128 over a length of 1/32 has zero resultant and a shear peak of 1 halfway through the patch. An overlay grid and the former moving-load station grid reported zero for that patch. Both the planar and spatial report regressions use a shorter patch with the same integrated peak.

The project report, critical-station results summary, and critical workbook maxima use the inward cuts at the member ends, both faces of interior jumps, and interior extrema. A point force applied at a fixed I support goes into the support reaction. Its exterior ordinate can exceed the forces that cross any cut within the member. The magnitude of two transverse components is evaluated from their squared-resultant polynomial, whose extrema can lie between the extrema of either component. If the assembled magnitude supplies only a bound, the project report refuses to state its extrema.

The point-query and diagram paths use the same stationary-point candidates, including boundary alias checks. They refuse when a distinct extremum rounds onto a boundary with a different ordinate. Compensated accumulation retains smaller actions when large actions cancel. In the unit-span moment example in #2437, the opposite uniform loads cancel and the remaining moment is (t(1-t)/2), with peak (1/8) at midspan. A spatial piece keeps its physical cut locations when rebased onto the parent member.

Rerun moving-load analyses and review member extrema in reports and saved diagrams for the affected models. See #2226, #2437 and the path endpoint follow-up on #1782.

An interval keeps the compensation of its coefficients until the force or moment is evaluated. This preserves a unit point jump next to a shear of 2^54 when the subsequent line load cancels the shear. A transverse resultant uses the same physical location and requested face for every component, including at rebased piece cuts. For an SRSS combination, the transverse resultant uses the squared sum of all underlying component polynomials. Critical summaries require exact extrema and refuse mixed ABS and SRSS expressions that only expose a bound.
