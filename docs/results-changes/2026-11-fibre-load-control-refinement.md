# Concrete fibre load-control refinement (2026-11-10)

Issue [#1576](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/issues/1576), results-change entry `fibre-load-control-refinement`.

The 2D fibre solver previously tried each prescribed load increment once and returned the last converged equilibrium when Newton failed. On origin/main at 99d5ee06c, the exact issue model returns load factors 0.6625, 0.7125 and 0.715625 with 80, 160 and 320 increments. All three explicitly report `stopped` and non-convergence; none is a capacity certificate. The issue's follow-up correctly distinguishes first concrete crushing from the later limit of these constitutive laws.

For fibre sections assigned to analysed frame elements and containing Hognestad concrete, failed trials now restore both displacements and material histories, bisect the increment, and retry the original target after a successful cutback. That retry is essential: a failure at the crushing kink must not become a permanent upper bound. Every trial tangent, including an equilibrium candidate, must factor as positive definite. An indefinite tangent is not solved with the LU fallback on this load-controlled path. The existing dense and sparse Cholesky implementations provide the same check. Unused section definitions and sections assigned only to truss or cable elements do not activate refinement. Other material-only runs and the 3D solver retain their existing stepping. The provenance registry uses the same assigned-section criterion; ordinary nonlinear frame projects receive no fibre-refinement notice.

After repeated cutbacks narrow the interval to at most a quarter of the requested tolerance in load-factor units, the solver returns the last converged state and `loadLimit` with lower and upper load factors, tolerance, reason, and `capacityCertified: false`. The existing equilibrium residual tolerance is unchanged; the load interval is a separate stopping criterion. No existing tolerance is relaxed. Input with zero increments, zero iterations or a non-positive/non-finite tolerance is refused. An attempt budget fails closed rather than claiming a capacity.

The interval brackets the numerical load-control boundary, not a mathematical upper bound on structural capacity. Iteration exhaustion, a material kink, numerical conditioning, a bifurcation or a limit point can stop continuation. Inadequate iteration limits can still stop before the actual limit. `lastConvergedLoadFactor` is the load carried by the returned equilibrium, and `status` remains `stopped` at such a boundary. Consumers must preserve this status and uncertainty; they must not relabel that number as a certified capacity. Unstable branches and later hardening branches require another continuation method. The repository's arc-length/displacement solvers assemble corotational elastic elements, not fibre sections, so routing this model to them would change its constitutive model.

With the same `max_iter = 40` for 80, 160 and 320 increments, the exact issue model now gives the same lower endpoint, 0.7171933591365814, at all three requested step counts. Its bracket widths are at most a quarter of the requested 1e-8 load-factor tolerance. Successful cutbacks are committed to `history`, whose length can exceed `n_increments`; that input remains the requested nominal step count. The independent 400-strip axial-equilibrium integration gives 400.19685653874325 kN m at extreme strain 0.004 and a scanned maximum of 435.869831150142 kN m through strain 0.02. Thus the issue's 133.399 kN reference is the first-crushing reference, not the maximum under its unbounded steel-hardening law. Load-increment convergence does not remove the displacement-based element's spatial discretization error: the base reaction moment decreases from 443.6342961611152 kN m at 20 elements to 440.9855661079287 kN m at 40 elements (the sparse path), approaching the independent section reference. No finite global capacity is claimed for the unbounded hardening law.

Reproduction and regression coverage live in `engine/tests/fiber_nonlinear_gate.rs`: the exact three step counts with a shared iteration budget, quarter-tolerance bracket width, the published load endpoint and section-reference moments, returned base reactions and member moments against the independent cantilever equilibrium M = lambda P L, committed final-state equality, equilibrium residual, independent moment-curvature and mesh-refinement checks, invalid controls, and a deliberately inadequate iteration budget. A completed Hognestad run reaches load factor 1 with no `loadLimit`. Dense and sparse steel-only regressions compare complete serialized results byte for byte before and after adding an unused concrete section. Existing steel, elastic, load recovery and 3D regressions remain unchanged. No safety advisory is added: the old engine explicitly reported a stopped run, and the example that called its last equilibrium a peak is absent from origin/main.
