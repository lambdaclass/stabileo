# CIRSOC 104 roof-step drift uses the receiving roof plane

Issues #2082; advisory [SA-2026-065](../advisories/SA-2026-065-snow-roof-step.md).
This correction is included in 0.6.1 (PR #2095). The advisory records the numerical
before/after witnesses on origin/main, also tagged v0.6.0.

CIRSOC 104 section 7.1 divides the receiving roof's p_f or p_s by
the density in expression (4) to obtain h_b. The drift's local height comes
from the upper and lower planes at the step station. Its surcharge is
superposed on balanced snow. Roof membership and positive plan overlap
prevent a neighboring source plane from replacing a receiver at a boundary.
Sliding uses its source plane's gradient, width and flat-roof snow load.

The shared roof model is in snow-roof-surfaces.ts. A non-collinear set of
coplanar geometric points supplies a plane; no inclination averaging or
nearest-rafter elevation projection remains. Collinear roof sections retain
their width-based extrusion behavior, while an isolated diagonal cannot
supply a surface over a two-dimensional footprint and refuses explicitly.

The calculation paths are the direct generator (projected width and panel
layouts), the CIRSOC load planner and the direct drift/sliding generator.
There is no Rust snow generator: linear/nonlinear, native/WASM solvers all
consume loads produced before that boundary. Tests therefore exercise the
generation entries in planar sections and spatial roof geometries without
running unrelated solver suites.

Tests retain the issue witnesses, the T123 skew plane, multiple rotations,
translation, mirroring, flat/no-step controls, both sides of the h_c/h_b
exemption and h_c = h_d cap, and small physical steps on either side of
the old geometric cutoff. No tolerance or existing baseline is loosened.

The results corpus adds a targeted loads group. It captures the production
snow generator through capture.ts/runGroup and is regenerated by
make snapshots-update FILE=src/lib/results-snapshot/__tests__/snapshot-loads.test.ts.
Every load piece has its own index so several loads on the same member
cannot overwrite one another in a snapshot. Existing solved-structure
snapshots have no generated snow inputs and are not rewritten.

The re-check registry conservatively selects generated snow projects and all their
derived quantities. Regenerate snow cases and re-run analyses, combinations,
envelopes and design checks; simply solving old generated loads again is
insufficient. The supporting code provisions are the repository extraction:
CIRSOC 104 chapters 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 and Figures 2 and 9.
