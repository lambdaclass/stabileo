# Compute quad self-weight from local edge differences

Quad self-weight integrates the four bilinear corner shape functions over a 2×2 Gauss rule. The surface Jacobian now uses differences along opposing edges. A translation of the model origin no longer inserts large coordinate terms that cancel in the Jacobian. The load builder and the accounting check use the same geometric samples. They sum those samples in different orders and compare the resulting forces and destinations at each corner. Comparing each corner also catches a redistribution that preserves total force and moment.

The rounding bound uses the number of additions in each corner sum, the load and combination multiplications, and the sum of the magnitudes of the quadrature contributions. Each corner's area contributions have the same sign, so the computed corner force magnitude supplies the scale. With binary64 unit roundoff `u` and `k` operations, each evaluation has a relative error bounded by `γ(k) = ku/(1 − ku)` against the common rounded samples. The two-sided comparison uses `2γ(k)/(1 − γ(k))` times the reference magnitude, with a separate allowance for subnormal multiplication. [Higham, The Accuracy of Floating Point Summation](https://doi.org/10.1137/0914050)

In the CAD arch fixture, quad 381 has parallel edges from nodes 1364 to 1435 and 1433 to 1434. Its area is the distance between those edges times the mean of their lengths. Multiplying that area by the fixture's density and thickness gives about 0.293563766846 kN of downward weight. The previous load accounting refused the fixture when its two calculations differed by about 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ kN. The isolated quad now gives the expected support reaction in linear, P-Delta and active-set solves. The full fixture still reaches the existing MITC4 distortion refusal on other quads.

Projects with spatial shell self-weight may see rounding-level changes in quad corner forces and calculated responses. The load accounting diagnostic identifies the corner if any force or couple component differs by more than its rounding bound.
