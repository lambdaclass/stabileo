# Moving axles at cumulative path joints

[#2101](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/issues/2101) and [#2104](https://github.com/lambdaclass/stabileo/issues/2104) reported warning-free shear errors in moving-load envelopes for a rotated beam with an inclined support and a mirrored beam with a tip spring.

At a sampled train position, subtracting a cumulative path distance before classifying the axle could create an unintended open interval between the axle and a joint. The interval carried a different shear from the adjacent span by the full axle force, and a rotation or reflection could determine which member received it. The engine now classifies the joint in cumulative path coordinates before forming a local member distance. The classifier accounts for cumulative joint roundoff and for the precision of the endpoint coordinates used to construct each segment. The sweep refuses a segment if its joint intervals overlap.

Each sweep carries the integer sample index into the station calculation for each axle. The calculation compensates for rounded multiplication and addition before testing the joint. An exactly computed sample keeps its geometric joint resolution regardless of the other axles’ offsets. A reversed interior station uses the member length minus the distance from the route entry to the axle on that member. This preserves coincidence with an opposing point load specified at the same local distance. Generated vehicle cases use the same browser sample grid and keep full sample precision.

The envelope includes the member-face limits for the train immediately before and immediately after a sampled joint. A connected joint takes the same nodal load in both limits. Force equilibrium at the cut adds the local axle force jump to the member face on the corresponding side. If an axle enters or leaves the route at the same sample, only the limit where it lies on the route includes it. The exact sampled position still includes the nodal loads at both endpoints. The sweep evaluates these limits at sampled joints and uses the specified grid for interior positions.

A simply supported span carrying a single 100 kN axle reaches a shear magnitude of 100 kN at a support face when the grid samples that support. The axle directly above a support produces a 100 kN reaction and zero member shear at the exact position. As the axle approaches from inside the span, the shear in the short interval between the support and the axle tends to the axle weight. This follows from vertical force and moment equilibrium. On a 10.1 m span with a 0.25 m step, the last axle station is 10.0 m. Moment equilibrium gives a right support reaction of 100 × 10.0 / 10.1 kN, about 99.01 kN, which governs the sampled shear at that face. The same force jump applies on either member adjoining a sampled internal joint. Native and browser sweeps project the axle onto the member axes and include the corresponding axial face limits. The planar browser sweep retains the signed axial projection on truss faces and suppresses the transverse component. For a truss rising 4 m over a 5 m length, a 10 kN downward axle projects to −8 kN along the member. With both ends restrained and only the two end stations sampled, the face limits are −8 kN at the start and +8 kN at the end. Spatial browser sweeps also keep the interior axial load at the axle station, so recovery includes the axial-force jump at the load.

The browser uses the same classifier for its planar and spatial sweeps and when generating vehicle position cases. An axle resolved to a joint becomes a nodal load in a saved position case, so subsequent analyses retain the joint identity. Saved cases represent the exact positions. The one-sided face limits are available in the moving-load sweep. Permanent member loads keep their specified stations.

The native regressions compare both stored nightly models with independent cantilever compatibility and equilibrium at cuts within each open axle interval and on both joint faces. They cover the prepared and constrained solvers. The browser regressions cover both WASM sweep exports, the planar synchronous and asynchronous sweeps, the spatial sweep, and applying an imported vehicle at the governing joint.

Re-run affected sweeps and regenerate saved vehicle position cases before using the resulting force extrema.
