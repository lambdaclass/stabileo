# Narrow partial member loads retain force and couple (2026-11-16)

Previously, the partial transverse-load kernel returned zero whenever the absolute interval width was below `1e-12`, irrespective of its resultant. Both 3D transverse planes called that kernel. Force and displacement recovery also skipped such spans. The equilibrium summary summed the assembled right-hand side, so the missing force was absent from the self-check's applied total too.

For an interval `[a,b]`, the force is the integral of the linear endpoint intensities, `qi*(b-a)/2 + qj*(b-a)/2`. Its first moment about the interval midpoint is `(qj-qi)*(b-a)^2/12`. The shared narrow-load conversion retains both integrals as a force at its resultant position and a residual couple. A zero-width interval with finite intensities has zero force and couple. Invalid intervals and non-finite resultants are refused before assembly, including prepared solves. The fixed-end-force and displacement-recovery paths share concentrated-force and couple operations with point loads; both force-diagram planes use the same interval integrator. A shrinking transverse force couple acts on the derivative of transverse displacement, which differs from a section couple when shear flexibility is present. The conversion retains that shear contribution in fixed-end forces and the corresponding displacement jump in recovery. First moments are computed about the interval start, retaining the residual couple when the centroid cannot be represented exactly. Diagram sampling and peak searches include the collapsed position and point-load neighbours.

Applied-force accounting now integrates the user's nodal, member and surface load definitions independently of equivalent nodal-force assembly. Existing residual and balance limits remain unchanged. A planted missing member load with a nonzero resultant fails static balance even when the assembled vector and reactions are all zero. Shell pressure, edge traction, body weight and thermal net force are accounted for directly from their definitions and geometry.

The issue's two-member witness has nodes at `x=0,3,6`, fixed outer ends, and a downward resultant of one on `[1.5,1.5000000000005]` of the first member. With the stiffness specified in the checked-in fixture, the former solution returned all zeros and passing balance diagnostics. The concentrated-load reference gives left reaction `27/32` and middle-node displacement `-0.0005625`. Tests derive the reference through point-load solutions as well as these witness values.

Affected outputs include reactions, displacements, end forces, interior force diagrams, member deflection and rotation, nonlinear results, and checks derived from them. Rerun models containing very narrow partial loads; review dependent designs. Ordinary full-width trapezoids are checked against the existing full-width kernel. Native and WASM regressions cover both spatial bending planes, linear and nonlinear entry points, width and interval boundaries, sign-reversing loads, and independent applied-force accounting.

This record assigns no safety advisory. Main/release exposure and generator reachability are documented in the worktree handoff for coordinator classification.


## Exact member endpoints (round 2, #2223)

Collapsed loads now use the same strict one-sided comparison in the shared distributed-load integrator: a load at `at` contributes when `at < x`, including when the station is the member's exact right endpoint. The former subtraction of `1e-10` excluded a load collapsed within that distance of either endpoint. For `L=3`, `[3-5e-13,3]` and uniform intensity `-1/(b-a)`, the integrated force at `t=1` is `-1`; the integrated moment about that section is `-(b-a)/2`. At `t=0`, both contributions are zero. The tests check these analytical contributions in 2D and both 3D planes, through native and WASM linear and nonlinear paths. An exact collapsed position takes the left limit; adjacent representable stations check both sides. Zero-width and reversed recovered intervals retain their previous empty-interval contribution, avoiding a panic in the new shared integration path.

The earlier local-axis regression incorrectly required a positive narrow load to return six exact zeros. Its replacement evaluates the closed-form cubic Hermite shapes on a member of length `6`, at the trapezoid centroid `2 + (5/9)*(b-a)`, multiplied by its force. At `x=2` those shape values are `[20/27, 8/9, 7/27, -4/9]`. The component error limit is `1e-13` times the force magnitude, stricter than the existing full-width control's absolute `1e-13`; the neglected second central moment is below this limit. The test additionally requires exact preservation of the transverse resultant and retains the zero-width and full-width controls.

The portal-frame and tower snapshots retain their committed answers; no snapshot update is required.

The inclined-support summary oracle now expects the directly specified transverse force `1000`, with no transverse contribution from the separately specified axial force. Its previous `1000 + cos(angle).mul_add(huge, -cos(angle)*huge)` included a support round-trip arithmetic residue. The assertion remains exact, including in the exported native/WASM case matrix.

The latter has unscoped declarations only for equilibrium summaries, diagnostic codes and solve success; it cannot excuse an unrelated change in member forces or displacements. It applies to models with member loads, shells or inclined supports, where independent load integration or support transformations can change the summaries. Negative legacy-corpus tests retain that protection.

Previously a reversed interval narrower than the conversion threshold could become a nonzero concentrated force, even though force diagrams treated that interval as empty. The review witness `a=1.5`, `b=a-5e-13`, `qi=qj=-1/(a-b)` now contributes no deflection or rotation, matching the existing force-diagram convention. Native and WASM recovery tests exercise both spaces, with and without shear flexibility, reversed widths on both sides of the conversion threshold, zero width, positive adjacent representable endpoints and ordinary valid spans. For the right-end interval `[3-5e-13,3]`, they compare directly against `-(b-a)/2` with error bounded by `1e-12` times that contribution's magnitude. This is substantially smaller than the contribution itself. Native and WASM assertion tests explicitly reject zero, so omitting the moment cannot pass. The pre-existing complete-response endpoint checks remain in place. This strengthens the test oracle; the endpoint moment was already present before round 3.

## Generated member endpoints and plastic validation (round 5, #2223)

The strict interval validator exposed callers that generated full-span bounds through independent length arithmetic. JavaScript influence loading used `Math.hypot` while the native validator used `sqrt(dx*dx + dz*dz)`. Plastic and Maxwell splits subtracted parent distances instead of deriving child endpoints from the child geometry. A full-span load could therefore extend one representable step beyond a child, and validation correctly refused it.

The planar influence and plastic callers now share geometric member lengths, exact endpoint encoding and distributed-load subdivision. A child endpoint is represented as the member end, and interior endpoints are mapped by their fraction of the source piece to its actual child length. Endpoint intensities follow the same parent interval. Positive load widths are not discarded by a subdivision tolerance. These remain member loads; no transverse load is replaced by nodal actions. External interval validation is unchanged.

The planar plastic loop now checks that kinematic rank analysis was actually available and the input valid before consulting its solvability verdict, at initialization, each event and final acceptance. Previously an invalid interval could be treated as a mechanism candidate and returned as an unresolved collapse history. Invalid and unavailable checks now raise their errors. After the original input has passed validation, a released node carrying a nodal moment can correctly become a mechanism even though the elastic validator reports that moment has no rotational stiffness. This case requires a second rank probe with only nodal moments set to zero: only if that probe validates successfully is the original state passed to the existing mechanism-work check. Distributed-load definitions remain in both probes; an invalid interval therefore still raises an error. The physical load history and mechanism work retain every nodal moment. Correctly partitioned models recover their mechanisms, factors and histories. This change is scoped to planar plastic analyses; native plastic and spatial analyses have not been modified.

Regression coverage retains all 23 generated CI witnesses and checks an inclined cantilever against integrated beam shear, bending and deflection. The same solution is checked on both children after splits at multiple stations, under rotations and mirroring. Planted invalid and unavailable rank checks must raise errors at every plastic acceptance path; ordinary verified-collapse and unresolved controls remain in force. The native buckling CI fixture used `x0 + h` for a node actually created with `(i+1)*h`. Its partition now uses the actual node coordinate and represents complete cell loads with implicit endpoints; its buckling assertion is unchanged.

The independent applied-load check checks net force only. A missing pure couple can pass that check when it is also absent from the assembled right-hand side. The existing assembly-based moment check and the physical member-response tests remain necessary. The changelog claim now states this limit explicitly; this round does not add independent moment integration.

No native engine production source changes in this round. The `planar-generated-span-endpoints` registry entry scopes changes to plastic factors, histories, mechanisms and solve success for planar frame models with a plastic analysis. It does not authorize changes to ordinary matrix results. Influence tools do not persist an analysis-kind stamp. Their generated loads and direct matrix references are corrected at the calling boundary.


## Compensated force totals and remapped load integrals (round 6, #2223)

Independent applied-force totals now use Neumaier compensated summation in the shared replacement routine. Loads ordered as a large positive force at one node, a unit force at another, and the cancelling negative force no longer lose the unit force from the summary. Assembly had retained that force and recovered its balancing reaction; ordinary input-order summation produced a false imbalance. Native and WASM tests cover planar and spatial linear, prepared/session, P–Delta and fiber entry points. Corotational solves do not report this equilibrium summary and are retained as correct-response controls. The existing force and wrench limits are unchanged. A separate registry entry covers summary, diagnostic and solve-status changes for nodal-only models as well as member-loaded models; it cannot authorize displacement or member-force changes.

The planar subdivision helper now preserves each source interval's resultant and first moment about the child origin. It keeps a representable distributed portion and integrates the difference between source and mapped widths and positions as concentrated corrections. A positive source interval whose endpoints map to the same double emits an interior point force and residual couple. The point remains at the mapped interior station, without snapping to a member end. Residual moments are computed relative to that station, so cancellation in the absolute first moment cannot erase a finite couple. For axial loads, an interior pair of opposite axial forces retains the first station moment without adding transverse force or a bending couple. Subnormal source widths are multiplied by their intensity before halving, retaining representable forces. An unrepresentable interior station or non-finite equivalent load is refused.

The exact witness uses source piece `[0,2]`, actual child length `2 - 2^-51`, interval `[1.5 - 2^-52,1.5]` and uniform intensity `2^52`. Previously both mapped endpoints were `1.5 - 2^-51`, and the unit resultant vanished. Tests isolate the residual couple as well as the force, include triangular and opposing intensities, and cover adjacent widths, both sides of geometric scaling, member ends, zero width and ordinary unchanged geometry. Real Maxwell and plastic splits reproduce the same length discrepancy using a member from `x=2.1` to `x=6.1` cut at parent distance `2`. Both callers share the corrected helper. There is no spatial frontend subdivision caller.

The new planar plastic registry entry requires partial distributed member loads and permits changes only to plastic factors, mechanisms, histories and solve status. Influence-tool results do not have persisted analysis stamps. The remapping defect is confined to this unmerged branch; this record adds no safety advisory.
