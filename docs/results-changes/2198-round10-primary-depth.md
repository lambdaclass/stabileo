# Primary shear uses the actual cage depth (2026-11-16)

PR #2198 round 10 corrects primary shear verification of provided beam and wall reinforcement when the selected cage diameter differs from the section's nominal `stirrupDia`. This error is also present on `origin/main` at `db3ebf092`: the beam generator can select a different diameter, and the provided-reinforcement verifier passes that cage's reinforcement area with an effective depth calculated from the nominal diameter. The existing pending `directional-stirrup-legs` registry entry covers this correction; its applicability and quantities are unchanged. Advisory decisions remain with the coordinator.

For the regression's 400 mm square section, 25 mm cover, single-row 16 mm longitudinal bars and a 12 mm cage replacing the nominal 8 mm cage, the actual primary depth is 400 − 25 − 12 − 16/2 = 355 mm. The previous calculation used 359 mm. It therefore overstated primary shear capacity and could accept shear exceeding the actual capacity. A smaller cage also received the wrong depth in the opposite direction. The geometry and independent capacity equations come from the existing round-6 regression, not an assumed engineering value.

Both directional depths now come from a single cage-bound geometry resolver. The primary depth uses the bottom layers and their final physical shift, including the existing depth tolerance. Every support, explicit end and span shear specification receives its resolved depth, and its primary tension-steel ratio uses that same depth. Capacity, minimum-shear thresholds, transverse spacing and concurrent torsion therefore consume consistent geometry. An incomplete arrangement with no bottom layers retains the existing 0.9h shear fallback and still receives a shear check; a missing cage continues to fail explicitly. Flexure, native strength equations, candidate selection, section sizing and search limits are unchanged.

Regressions cover both strength editions, both axis mappings, beams and walls, direct verification and the design adapter, global cages, shared regional cages and differing explicit end cages. Diameters below, equal to and above the nominal diameter are checked with and without final geometry shifts. Demands below, at and above actual shear capacity exercise acceptance and failure. Actual-depth spacing boundaries also retain passing and failing cases. The round-6 solved regression now uses the actual cage in its planar magnitude expectation and exercises primary spatial shear as well as secondary spatial shear, linear and P-Delta solves, both strength editions and grouped/compact station transfer. The shared native section kernel runs through WASM; there is no separate native provided-cage geometry entry point.

The generated-cage witness uses the supported 2025 candidate generator and verifies its selected cage under each strength edition. The 2005 generator already refuses unsupported spacing and is not used to manufacture a cage. No tolerance, baseline, assertion limit or performance allowance changes. The c6 torsion blocker (#2225) and cage-option false refusals (#2237) remain deferred.

Round 11 checks the existing design–detailing feedback-loop expectation against its own geometry: a 550 mm beam, 25 mm cover, 8 mm actual cage and single-row 10 mm bottom bars, raised 10 mm with 15 mm depth tolerance, give d = 550 − 25 − 8 − 10/2 − 10 − 15 = 487 mm. The row-1 spacing limit is d/2 = 243.5 mm = 24.35 cm. The cage-bound subtraction order represents this as 24.349999999999998 cm, which the existing one-decimal formatter reports as 24.3 cm; the previous association represented it as 24.350000000000001 cm and reported 24.4 cm. Only the test’s expected reported value changes in round 11, with its tolerance retained. The unrounded geometry agrees with the hand calculation, and the spacing failure and repair remain unchanged. The existing registry coverage of actual-depth primary shear spacing applies.
