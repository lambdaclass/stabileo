# Pure torsion and secondary shear distribution (2026-11-16)

PR #2198 round 9 corrects a false refusal introduced on the pending directional-stirrup branch. The existing `directional-stirrup-legs` registry entry covers this change and now explains it in all seven locales. Its applicability and quantities remain unchanged. No new advisory is created.

Active torsion creates a secondary-direction shear result even without secondary shear, so that the outer closed-hoop legs remain available for combined reinforcement accounting. That result previously applied the across-width shear leg-distribution requirement to zero secondary shear. A 300 × 600 mm rectangular member with the two outer secondary legs consequently failed that shear check despite adequate torsion reinforcement.

The single directional shear evaluator now applies its distribution requirement to secondary shear only when its checked demand is nonzero. Primary distribution checks retain their previous behavior. The secondary result, effective leg count, reinforcement area, combined shear/torsion allocation, outer-leg capacity, longitudinal torsion allocation and torsion spacing check remain available. The fix changes the applicability of a shear check, not its required count or acceptance limit.

The numerical witness uses the section, materials and geometry already exercised in `issue-2142-torsion.test.ts`: b = 0.3 m, h = 0.6 m, fc = 25 MPa, fy = 420 MPa, cover = 0.025 m and 8 mm stirrups. The new regression uses two legs in each direction at 0.1 m, moment 20 kN·m, torque 10 kN·m, zero axial force and shear, and an explicit longitudinal torsion allocation of 100 cm². Against `121925115`, secondary shear and overall verification fail. After this correction both are `ok`, with the torsion check still active. The test independently computes the one-leg torsion area from the closed-hoop geometry and checks the actual available outer-leg area exactly. Four secondary legs already pass and remain a control.

Regressions cover both editions, both flexural axes, beams, columns and walls, and direct, prepared and concurrent verification. Signed secondary shear below, at and above the existing 0.01 kN verification-input cutoff, and near actual zero, still requires four distributed legs; two legs still fail. A secondary result needed for active torsion now reads its shear from the original concurrent demand, so the reporting cutoff cannot turn a nonzero demand into the zero-shear exemption. The existing cutoff for creating a standalone secondary check is unchanged. Tests below, at and above the torsion hoop-spacing boundary retain the exact original limit. Real spatial linear and P-Delta WASM solves exercise grouped and compact transfer through automatic, unified and manual design wrappers. Planar linear/P-Delta solves remain answered controls because their formulation has no torsional degree of freedom. There is no native directional-cage or torsion verifier; the shared section strength evaluator used by these checks runs through WASM, and existing native recheck disclosures remain unchanged.

Round 9 also strengthens the seismic end-cage tests: each end resolves its explicit cage or shared fallback, both effective cages receive an independent strength check, and an inadequate shared fallback must be updated. Suppressing only the shared-cage update makes four cases fail. No seismic writer behavior changes. Spanish, Portuguese, German, French and Turkish notices now retain the safety warnings about zero-torsion shear verification, column tie distribution, distinct beam end cages and withholding constructibility for unverified horizontal crossties. German uses “Bügelschenkel” throughout the title and explanation.

The deferred #2225 and #2214 checks, candidate enumeration (#2237), load allocation, search bounds, baselines and tolerances are outside this correction.
