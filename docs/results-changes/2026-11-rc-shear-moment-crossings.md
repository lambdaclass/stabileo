# Curtailed beam shear at moment transitions (2026-11-21)

A span shear check can be governed by a cut between solver stations when the demand crosses the hogging threshold while substantial shear is still acting. The station at the start of the interval can be sagging and the station at the end can have negligible shear. With curtailed reinforcement, the steel that remains at the interior cut can justify the assumed effective depth and still provide less concrete shear resistance than the bottom cage.

Verification and candidate ranking now include cuts on both sides of that moment threshold for each combination where the existing retained-layout check applies. At retained-layout cuts inside an interval that brackets the threshold, the shear demand is the larger absolute shear at the two bounding stations of that combination. Under distributed load, the moment can cross the threshold earlier than the chord between the station moments indicates, at a higher shear. The endpoint demand avoids reducing the check demand to the shear interpolated at the estimated crossing. The same retention, depth, and transverse cage checks apply. A supported hogging cut uses the retained top steel area in [CIRSOC 201-2025 Table 22.5.5.1](../codes/CIRSOC/markdown/cirsoc-201-2025/capitulo-22-resistencia-de-las-secciones-de-los-elementos.md#c22.5.5.1), row (c). The same formula and the existing drawn-reinforcement evidence requirements apply. A formerly passing span shear row can now fail at the interior cut.

A short bar gap can end before the chord predicts that the bars enter tension. The cutoff check now treats the full interval between the stations bracketing a crossing as potentially requiring that face. When the station shear bound is active and the top layout cannot substantiate the assumed effective depth in any overlapping cut, the shear verdict is withheld. The same interval rule applies to a bottom-bar cutoff in a support region where the concrete shear check needs the tension steel and the retained bars cannot substantiate the credited area or depth. Provided reinforcement verification and both candidate ranking paths use these checks.

A refused primary seismic beam or column now shows its applicable capacity-shear and detailing checks as unevaluated in the report, with the reason for the refusal in each row.

Re-run affected reinforced concrete member checks before issuing an updated report.
