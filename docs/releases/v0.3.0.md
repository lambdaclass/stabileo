# Stabileo 0.3.0: more of the solver checked, and every wrong result published

**English** · [Español](v0.3.0.es.md) · [Português](v0.3.0.pt.md)

Released 2026-10-21. The full technical record is in [`CHANGELOG.md`](../../CHANGELOG.md); the
advisories are in [`docs/advisories/`](../advisories/README.md).

> **Note on timing.** stabileo.com deploys every change merged into `main`, so the fixes below
> went live between 19 and 21 October 2026, as each was merged. The app shows, once, a notice that
> results may have changed, and projects saved before a change that concerns them show a re-check
> prompt when they are opened.

Since 0.2.0 we kept checking the solver: an independent re-assembly of frame and truss models, symbolic derivations of each element, comparisons with other programs, physical invariants
on generated models. That found sixteen more defects that could give a wrong result. They are fixed, and each one has a published safety advisory saying what was wrong, who
is affected and what to re-check.

---

## 1. Your results may change: re-run your projects

Re-run the projects that use the features below and compare with the results you already used.

**Also changed (not a defect fix, but numbers move):** a rigid diaphragm now makes every node of
the floor rotate with its master, as SAP2000, ETABS and OpenSees do. Floors were too soft in
torsion and column end torques came out zero (#342).

## 2. Warned or refused instead of silently wrong

- **Known issues are named on the result.** A result that an open defect could affect carries a
  warning naming it, in your language. One remains: material-nonlinear analysis is a whole-member
  secant model, so its response jumps at first yield (#428).
- **Assumed defaults are said.** A member temperature load without the material's α or the
  section's depth is solved with the old defaults and says so.
- **New refusals:** a nodal moment on a joint where every member is hinged and no support, connector or constraint
  takes rotation (nothing can carry it);
  `leftHand: true` member axes; a shell load that names an element of another type; a soil curve
  without the inputs its reference needs.

## 3. Checked more deeply

- **End-to-end certificates** for frames and trusses, 2D and 3D: an independent checker rebuilds
  the stiffness and the loads from the model itself, in interval arithmetic and with its own
  formulas, and certifies the displacements against the model, not only against the matrices the
  engine assembled. It already caught three cases where the engine and the model disagreed.
- **The same model always gives the same numbers,** bit for bit, and every report, workbook and
  CSV records how it was produced (app and engine version, model and results hashes). Projects
  saved before a change that concerns them open with a re-check prompt.
- **Concrete design (CIRSOC 201) from the engine:** sections, interaction diagrams and slabs come
  from one implementation in the engine; the TypeScript code it replaced is kept only to test it.
- **Mesh error estimates** for plates, shells and solid shells (labelled as estimates, never as
  certified).
- **More tests:** physical invariants on generated models (rotation, relabelling, symmetry,
  reciprocity, energy), every large-model solve path, an acceptance suite from published
  benchmarks, and a reviewed list of the code the certificates trust.

## 4. Known limitations

- Material-nonlinear analysis is a whole-member secant model (#428).
- A cable's unstressed length is the chord as drawn: a heavy member modelled as a cable hangs with
  a large tension even unloaded. Model heavy members as trusses; a warning and a pretension input
  are planned (#527).
- When 3D plastic collapse finds no consistent set of hinges it stops at a safe but very
  conservative load factor and says so.
- A single free curved-shell element whose normals vary keeps a small thermal stress (0.5–2 % of
  the restrained value), shrinking as the mesh is refined.
- The results workbook and CSV still leave curved-shell moments blank.
- Old arcs whose members carry their own loads, releases or offsets are not re-oriented
  automatically; set their axes by hand (SA-2026-016).
