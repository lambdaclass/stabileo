### Performance
#### Linear solves and the gravity load plan cost what they did in October again (2026-11-12)

Since the November safety work a linear solve took 1,5 to 8 times longer than at the end of October (45 times on a 60-DOF frame), every load case of a combination solve paid 15 times its own cost in screens, and the gravity load plan grew with members times panels: 0,78 to 1,5 s for a 4860-member building instead of 0,22 s. No result changes; the same checks run and give the same answers.

- The small-displacement screen's span rotations are decided with one window per load interval (the exact maximum in 2D; in 3D an upper bound that settles every member within the limit, and only a member beyond it is measured with the 64 windows), its cubic roots and candidate points use fixed arrays instead of allocations, a case without thermal loads does not scan its loads once per member, and the second-order screen looks member lengths up once. Per load case on a 1100-member 2D frame: 12,8 ms to 2,3 ms; on a 220-member 3D frame: 4,8 ms to 0,7 ms.
- The dense Jacobi spectrum of the stability verdict (models under the sparse threshold) stops when a sweep rotates nothing above the rounding of its own rows and columns, the relative rule of Demmel and Veselić, instead of running its 64 sweeps every time: ten sweeps on a 60-DOF frame, the verdict from 5,3 ms to about 2 ms.
- The gravity load planner keeps each level's panels and members, and the roof members, on a grid of their boxes, so the headroom probe, the scan for members beside a probe and the coverage lookup read the few near the point instead of all of them, and distances are compared squared: the 1730-member building plans in 43 ms (panels) and 74 ms (width) instead of 118 and 232, and a 4860-member one in 0,16 and 0,25 s instead of 0,78 and 1,5 s (0,22 s at the end of October). The same panels, strips and notes come out.
- The 3D prepared solve builds its known-issue list once instead of twice.
- `cargo bench --bench workflow_bench` runs again: its flat plate is held in plane at a second corner, as the perf gate's plate is, instead of being refused as a mechanism.

New gates hold these, in units of a fixed calibration workload timed on the same thread rather than milliseconds, so they hold on a slow or loaded runner: ten prepared load cases of a 600-member 2D frame within 4,5 calibrations and of a 220-member 3D frame within 3 (12,2 and 9,0 before, 1,6 and 1,1 after), the planner's 4860-member building within 75 (panels) and 110 (width) times its own cost on a 204-member one (111 and 173 before, 43 and 46 after), and a 60-DOF frame's spectrum within 16 Jacobi sweeps (64 before).
