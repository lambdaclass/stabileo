### Fixed

#### The seven-storey PRO example stands its core on its whole base, and the sheets read its walls a storey at a time (2026-11-15)

Available when the unified PRO plates build switch is enabled. The production build keeps it off.

The core's walls of `pro-edificio-7p` rested on the corner column's support alone: the three other base nodes were held by nothing, so the walls hung from the floors, the reinforcement sheets failed them for a base not held all along, and the back wall read no forces at all. The card now holds every base node of a wall that stands on the foundation, as the other foundation nodes are held. Coplanar shells are no longer made one plate across a line where a slab meets them, as they already were not across a beam: the back wall of the core, which no beam crosses, was one plate seven storeys tall, kept its coarse shells as a "real mesh" and came out of the sheets as one storey with a 21.6 m vertical bar. With no grid, the sheets now cut a wall at the elevations the physical members span. Diagnostics take a node tied by a constraint as connected, and no longer name the free tip of a slab held along a whole side (a balcony); a wall's unsupported foot is still named. A cantilever panel's step-by-step says it is a cantilever, as its table does. In the sheets, "Support the whole base" holds every wall on the foundation at once, solves again and designs again; a wall on a floor above is told to share nodes with what is below instead of being given a support in mid-air, and an unheld base is offered no thickness. With no results, the sheets' window offers "Solve and design" instead of a button that did nothing. On the example, after meshing at the solve, the sheets go from one failing slab and three failing walls to none.
