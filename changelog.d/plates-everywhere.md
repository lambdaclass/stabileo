### Changed
#### Every slab, wall and shell in PRO is a plate, meshed when you choose (2026-11-09)

Plates drawn by their outline could be selected whole and meshed after they were made, with the mesh dialog's free, rectangular, square or triangular elements and the nodes meeting the plates beside them; the rest of the model's shells could not: an example, an older project, an IFC import, shells drawn corner by corner, and the slabs and walls of the building tool, which were meshed by a separate "mesh at solve" path. Now they all behave the same, with no step to take: in PRO every shell no plate holds becomes one as it comes in, its outline and holes its boundary and its shells its current mesh, so the analysis answer does not change until it is meshed again. Loads written on the shells stay as they were; a uniform one on every shell of a slab becomes the plate's own load when the slab is meshed again, so it follows the new mesh. Selecting one or more plates shows a card that opens the mesh dialog for them; the quick editor offers it for the plate of the shell it is open on. A meshed plate is drawn as the piece until there are results, and with its mesh after (the plate list's switch overrides it). The building tool places its slabs and walls as plates, meshed now or by the analysis; a slab plate's mesh keeps the columns' sections in it (lines on their faces, the nodes inside tied to the column), a waffle slab is meshed no coarser than its ribs, and mesh nodes on an outline side whose two corners carry the same plain support get it too. Meshing several plates together leaves every one current. The old mesh generator is gone; a circular plate, with a circular hole if wanted, is drawn from the plate card like the other shapes, and the curved surfaces (cylinder, cone, spherical cap and zone, hyperboloid, hyperbolic paraboloid) are in Generators. Plates are named L1, L2… for slabs and M1, M2… for walls, as the building tool and the physical elements name them, and carry the levels they span. Curvature, the mid-surface offset and foundation springs given to a plate selected whole are kept on it and given to every new mesh. A quad whose corners leave one plane by more than 1 mm is solved as a curved shell without a box to tick, and can be flattened in Specifications. A floor load can be put on a slab plate before it has a mesh.
